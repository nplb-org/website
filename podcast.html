The No Patient Left Behind Podcast

An Emergency Medicine Cases podcast series hosted by Dr. Anton Helman, with Dr. Grant Innes and invited experts.

Emergency department crowding is one of the most pressing challenges facing health care systems today. Crowding is not simply an emergency department problem. It is a symptom of broader health system pressures that affect patient care, access, safety, and the experience of health care professionals.

In this eight-part podcast series, emergency medicine leaders, clinicians, and health system experts explore practical, evidence-informed strategies to improve patient flow, address access block, strengthen accountability, and build a more effective health care system.

The goal is to move beyond describing the problem toward identifying solutions that make a meaningful difference for patients and the teams caring for them.

Episodes

Episode 1 — coming early 2027

Are We Solving the Wrong Problems?

Why are emergency departments more overcrowded than ever despite decades of attempted solutions? In this inaugural episode, Drs. Anton Helman and Grant Innes challenge conventional thinking about emergency department overcrowding. They explain why diverting low-acuity patients to urgent care centres and virtual care may do little to improve access for seriously ill patients, how hospital and community care failures contribute to emergency access block, and why simply adding more resources is not always the answer.

Hosts

Dr. Anton Helman

Host

Founder, host and director of Emergency Medicine Cases, the top emergency medicine education podcast in Canada. Staff physician at North York General in Toronto and assistant professor in the Division of Emergency Medicine, Department of Family and Community Medicine, University of Toronto.

Dr. Grant Innes

Co-host

Nationally respected emergency physician and health system leader. Over nearly four decades he has shaped emergency medicine in Canada through clinical work in Vancouver and Calgary, leadership as Chair of the Vancouver Emergency Services Council and Emergency Medicine Department Head for the Calgary Region and the University of Calgary, and national contributions through CAEP and the Canadian Journal of Emergency Medicine.
